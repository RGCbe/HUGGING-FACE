# The Pig War (1859): sources

- **NPS**: US National Park Service, San Juan Island National Historical Park, "The Pig War",
  https://www.nps.gov/sajh/learn/historyculture/the-pig-war.htm
- **WP**: Wikipedia, "Pig War (1859)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_War_(1859)

| Fact in the script | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Border treaty (Oregon Treaty) signed June 15, 1846; the water boundary "the middle of the channel" was unclear, two channels (Haro and Rosario Straits) with the San Juan Islands between | WP |
| Hudson's Bay Company sheep farm on San Juan Island, run by Charles Griffin, an Irishman | WP, NPS |
| 25 to 29 American settlers by mid-1859 ("around twenty-five") | WP |
| June 15, 1859: Lyman Cutlar, an American farmer, shot a pig rooting in his garden and eating his tubers (potatoes); it was Griffin's | WP, NPS |
| Exactly 13 years after the treaty (both June 15) | WP |
| Cutlar offered $10, Griffin wanted $100; Cutlar said the pig was trespassing on his land | WP |
| British authorities threatened to arrest Cutlar; the settlers asked for US Army protection | WP |
| Captain George Pickett landed July 27, 1859 with 64 (NPS) or 66 (WP) soldiers: "about sixty-five" | NPS, WP |
| Pickett: "We'll make a Bunker Hill of it" | WP |
| Governor James Douglas sent warships | WP, NPS |
| 461 Americans with 14 cannons; five British warships with 70 guns and 2,140 men (WP; NPS counts 52 guns, so the script follows WP and says "within a few weeks", not a date) | WP |
| Rear Admiral Robert Baynes refused to "involve two great nations in a war over a squabble about a pig" | NPS, WP |
| President Buchanan sent General Winfield Scott, who arrived in October 1859; joint occupation with token forces of no more than 100 men | WP, NPS |
| British camp in the north, American camp in the south; 12 years of joint occupation | WP, NPS |
| Soldiers visited each other's camps, celebrated holidays, held athletic competitions; park rangers name alcohol as the biggest threat to peace | WP |
| Treaty of Washington (1871) sent the question to Kaiser Wilhelm I; a three-man commission met in Geneva for nearly a year; ruled for the United States on October 21, 1872 | WP, NPS |
| Royal Marines left November 25, 1872 ("a month later") | WP, NPS |
| No human casualties; the only casualty was the pig | WP, NPS |

Left out: the "potatoes" exchange ("it was eating my potatoes" / "it is up to you to keep your potatoes
out of my pig"), which WP calls likely apocryphal.
