[warm] Long ago, on Mount Kailash, Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati sat together with their two sons. The elder was Ganesha, wise and gentle, with his elephant head and his big round belly. The younger was Murugan, young and quick, who rode a beautiful peacock.
[mysterious] One day the wandering sage Narada came to Kailash, playing his veena and singing the name of God. In his hands he carried something very special. A golden fruit. This is no ordinary fruit, he said. This is the gnana pazham, the fruit of wisdom.
[warm] Narada placed the fruit before Lord Shiva. Shiva smiled and thought, I have two sons, so I will cut it in two and give each of them half. But Narada shook his head. This fruit cannot be cut, he said. Its wisdom must go, whole, to one.
[curious] So who should have it? Both brothers wanted it. And so a contest was set. Whoever goes around the whole world three times, and comes back first, shall have the fruit of wisdom.
[excited] Murugan did not wait a moment. He jumped onto his peacock, and the peacock spread its shining wings and flew. Over mountains and forests, over rivers and over the seas, Murugan raced around the world as fast as the wind.
[warm] And Ganesha? Ganesha did not move. His mount was a little mouse. How could a mouse race a peacock around the world? So Ganesha sat quietly and thought. What is my world? Who gave me my life, and everything I have?
[warm] Then he stood up, folded his hands, and slowly walked around his father and mother, Shiva and Parvati. Once. Twice. Three times. Then he bowed to them and said, my mother and father are my whole world, and I have gone around my world three times.
[warm] Shiva and Parvati were filled with joy at their son's wisdom. And the fruit of wisdom was given to Ganesha.
[serious] Just then the wind rose, and Murugan landed back on Kailash, tired and proud, with the dust of the whole world on his feet. And there, in his brother's hands, he saw the fruit.
[sad] Murugan's heart sank. All that flying, and the prize was already gone. He felt that he had been cheated. Without a word he turned away from Kailash. He took off his fine clothes and his jewels, and he left.
[mysterious] He went south, far to the south, to a quiet hill in Tamil Nadu. There he stood alone, his head shaved, wearing only a simple cloth, with just a wooden staff in his hand. The young prince had become a young monk.
[warm] His mother and father came to him on that hill. And as the story is told, they said to him gently, my child, why do you need a fruit? You yourself are the fruit. Pazham nee. You are the fruit of wisdom. In another telling, it is the great poet Avvaiyar who sings those words to him.
[warm] Many people say that is how the hill got its name. Pazham nee. Palani. And Murugan stayed. Even today, on top of the Palani hill, he stands as Dhandayudhapani, the one who holds the staff, just as he stood that day. And many devotees shave their heads there, to be like him.
[warm] And the story has a sweet ending. At Palani, devotees receive panchamirtham, a sweet made of fruits, honey and jaggery. It is said that Ganesha himself first made it, to make peace with his little brother after the race.
[warm] So what does this story teach us? Ganesha shows us that our mother and father are our world. And Murugan shows us that we do not need to win the prize, because the wisdom we are looking for is already inside us.
