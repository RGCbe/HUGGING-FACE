After the First World War, Australia gave land to its old soldiers. The idea was simple: you fought for your country, now go and farm. Nice idea, right? So in Western Australia, in an area called Campion, these former soldiers started growing wheat. Everything was going fine... until nineteen thirty-two.
In nineteen thirty-two, around twenty thousand emus walked into the area. If you've never seen an emu, it's a huge bird. It can't fly. But it can run. Very fast. These emus ate the wheat, ruined the crops, and broke big holes in the fences. And through those holes, rabbits came in as well. Double trouble for the farmers.
So what did the farmers do? Remember, they were former soldiers. They went to the government and said: we need machine guns. The Defence Minister, Sir George Pearce, said yes. The army sent Major Meredith, two soldiers, two Lewis machine guns, and ten thousand bullets. The plan was simple. Shoot the emus. Problem solved. Just like a plan from your manager in a Monday meeting. Perfect on paper. [chuckle]
November second, nineteen thirty-two. The war begins. The soldiers spot the emus. They get the guns ready. And the emus? They split into small groups and run out of range. That move was not in the army's plan. Two days later, more than a thousand emus gathered near a dam. This was the perfect chance! The army was ready. They started shooting... and got just twelve emus. Then? The gun jammed. And the rest of the emus? Bye bye. Gone.
By November eighth, the army had fired two thousand five hundred bullets. The results? Very poor. The newspapers started making fun of the army. So on November eighth, the army pulled out. Round one: the emus win.
But the story doesn't end there. The farmers asked again, and on November thirteenth, the army came back. Round two. This time it went a bit better. The fighting went on until December tenth. In his report, Major Meredith said: nine hundred and eighty-six emus, nine thousand, eight hundred and sixty bullets. Do the maths. That's exactly ten bullets for every emu. Ten bullets. For one bird. [laugh]
And here's what Major Meredith said about the emus: They can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks. In simple words: these birds stood in front of machine guns like tanks. An army major, comparing a bird to a tank. That's the level of this war. In parliament, one senator even called the Defence Minister the Minister of the Emu War.
So, back to the question from the start. What happened next? Who won? And the answer is... the emus. The farmers asked for army help three more times: in nineteen thirty-four, in nineteen forty-three, and one last time in nineteen forty-eight. Every time, the government said no. The army never came back. In the end, the farmers used bounty rewards and strong fences instead. Not machine guns.
