It starts with a border. In eighteen forty-six, America and Britain agreed on a border in the north-west of America. In the water, the line would go through the middle of the channel. Simple, right? Just one problem. There were two channels. And right between them sat a group of islands. The biggest one was called San Juan.
So both countries said: this island is ours. The British Hudson's Bay Company had a big sheep farm there, run by an Irishman called Charles Griffin. And by eighteen fifty-nine, around twenty-five American settlers had arrived as well. Neighbours, on one small island. What could go wrong?
June fifteenth, eighteen fifty-nine. An American farmer called Lyman Cutlar walks into his garden, and finds a pig eating his potatoes. Cutlar picks up his gun, and shoots the pig. And whose pig was it? Charles Griffin's. Fun fact: that was exactly thirteen years after the border treaty was signed. Happy anniversary.
Cutlar offered Griffin ten dollars for the pig. Griffin said no. He wanted one hundred dollars. Cutlar refused. He said the pig was on his land, so why should he pay? Just like two neighbours fighting over a mango tree on the wall between their houses.
Then the British said they would arrest Cutlar. So the American settlers asked their army for help. And the army sent Captain George Pickett, with about sixty-five soldiers. On July twenty-seventh, they landed on the island, and Pickett said: We'll make a Bunker Hill of it. Bunker Hill was a famous battle between America and Britain. And this one? Over a pig.
The British governor, James Douglas, was not happy. He sent a warship. Then more warships. Within a few weeks, there were four hundred and sixty-one American soldiers with fourteen cannons on the island. And facing them, five British warships, with seventy guns and two thousand one hundred and forty men. All because of one pig.
The governor wanted the American soldiers gone. But the British admiral, Robert Baynes, refused to attack. He said he would not start a war between two great nations, over a squabble about a pig. Finally, someone sensible.
In America, President James Buchanan sent his top general, Winfield Scott, to calm things down. In October, Scott arrived, and the two sides made a deal. Both could keep soldiers on the island, but only a small force of no more than one hundred men.
So the British camped in the north of the island, and the Americans camped in the south. And for the next twelve years, they waited. But here's the funny part. The soldiers visited each other's camps. They celebrated holidays and held sports competitions together. And today, the rangers on the island say the biggest danger to peace was not the guns. It was the alcohol.
So who decided who owned the island? Not America. Not Britain. In eighteen seventy-one, they asked the German Emperor, Kaiser Wilhelm the First. Yes, a German emperor, settling a fight between America and Britain that started with a pig.
His three experts met in Geneva for almost a year. And on October twenty-first, eighteen seventy-two, they decided: the island belongs to America. A month later, the British marines packed up and left.
So who won? America got the island. But in the whole Pig War, not one soldier was killed. Not one sailor. The only one who lost his life... was the pig.
