# How Manoj talks: script rules for every video

The voice is the show. A script is Manoj telling a friend a story over tea, not an article read
aloud. These rules are for every script: Tea Break history videos, manga recaps, Shorts, TikToks.

## The test

Say each line out loud. If you wouldn't say it that way to a friend at a tea stall, rewrite it.

## Ten rules

1. **Contractions, always:** it's, he's, don't, can't, that's, they'd, wasn't.
   "It is the year twenty thirty-one." becomes "So it's twenty thirty-one."
2. **Mix the rhythm.** A longer line that carries the story, then a short punch. Never three short
   sentences of the same shape in a row ("This is Arjun. He is eighteen. Business is slow.").
3. **Link the facts the way people talk:** so, and then, but here's the thing, turns out, which
   means, now, because, and of course. A story flows from one thing to the next; it doesn't start
   over with a new subject in every sentence.
4. **React like a person.** About one honest reaction or small opinion a minute: "Honestly? I'd
   have run too." "Bold move." "This is my favourite part." Manoj's take is what makes the video
   his, and it is the human input YouTube looks for when it decides what can earn.
5. **Talk to the viewer now and then:** "Imagine you're the general...", "You can guess what
   happened next.", a real question at a turning point, then a pause.
6. **Tell the story, don't describe the picture.** The picture already shows Viktor laughing. The
   voice says why it matters or how it feels: not "Viktor points at Arjun and laughs." but "And of
   course Viktor has to make it a show."
7. **Concrete beats general:** the real number, the real object, the exact words someone said.
8. **Jokes:** set it up straight, put the funny word last, then stop (the music drops there).
   Never explain a joke. Call back to an earlier joke near the end.
9. **A turn every 30 to 40 seconds:** a new question, a reversal, a surprise, a small cliffhanger.
10. **Fresh words every video.** The only fixed sentence is the greeting, "Hello friends, I'm
    Manoj." Nothing else is copied from video to video: no "In this video, I'll tell you...",
    no stock ending. End on a take or a question about this story.

## Manoj's kind of joke

These are lines Manoj wrote and recorded himself. They are the model for every joke in his voice:

- "This war was shorter than my tea break. Some of my office meetings last longer than this whole war!"
- "In eighteen thirty-five, people believed in bat-men on the Moon. Today we have WhatsApp forwards.
  Honestly, not much has changed!"
- "Mike lived for eighteen months without a head. On Monday mornings, I feel exactly like Mike!"
- "The CIA spent a fortune to turn a cat into a spy. Anyone who owns a cat could have told them:
  cats never do what you ask!"

The pattern: a true fact from the story, then the same thing in a busy working person's everyday
life (the office, Monday mornings, the commute, WhatsApp groups, the neighbours, the shared water
tank, chai). Put three or four of these in every video, in his "I" and "my": that is what makes it
his, and nobody else's. Laugh with people, never at them.

## Never

"dive in", "game-changer", "unlock", "let's get into it", "little did they know", "but then, he
discovers a shocking secret" (name the secret instead), "in conclusion", "it is the year", "here
is", chains like "This is X. He is Y.", or reading a speech bubble word for word and then
describing the same picture.

## AI habits to cut (found in our own drafts)

These read fine on paper and sound like a machine out loud. Say the thing instead:

- Signposts: "Things get interesting when...", "Think about that for a second.", "Think about what this
  really means.", "The irony is almost too good.", "Here's the thing." Just say the interesting thing.
- "That's not X anymore. That's Y." ("That's not a drink anymore. That's a weapon.")
- Polished comparisons: "nowhere near as impressive as his reputation", "basically a gold mine",
  "at the bottom of the food chain", "on a completely different level", "could change the whole world".
- A tidy summary closing every section, and grand endings: "a mystery that has only just begun".
- Three neat clauses in a row ("He hasn't learned a new skill, nobody gave him a weapon, and nothing
  should make him this strong") when one honest reaction does the job ("B-Rank Hunters don't just do that.").

What a person does instead: an opinion in "I" ("which I love, by the way", "to be fair to him, I'd think
I was special too"), a tiny everyday detail ("same traffic, same noise"), a fragment ("Rank F. That's the
floor."), a reminder to the viewer ("Remember he said that."), and letting a line end flat.

## Manga reviews: explain it like a friend

The owner's rule: never read out what is written in the manga. Explain it: what the world is, why each
character does what they do, why a moment matters, what a twist means for later, and what Manoj thinks
of it. Quote a character only for the line that lands. Every review gets YouTube chapters ("chapter"
in recap.json).

## Dialogue in manga recaps

Quote one or two lines that matter per scene, with attitude and who said it ("and Viktor, being
Viktor, goes: tea from an F-Rank? Is it poisoned with weakness?"). Say the rest in your own words.
The bubbles are on screen; the voice adds the attitude, not a second reading.

## Writing for the AI voice without sounding like a robot

- Punctuation is direction: "..." is a pause before a reveal, a dash is a sudden turn, a question
  mark is a real question.
- One idea per sentence carries the stress; put that word last ("...and the one who walked in was
  his father.").
- Numbers are written as they are said ("twenty thirty-one", "ten rupees").
- The voice can swallow a tiny first word ("He", "A", "And") at the start of a sentence that
  begins a new piece. Join that sentence to the one before with a comma or "and", or start with
  the name.
- If the listening test fails a line, rephrase it the way a person would say it. Don't chop it
  into shorter sentences; that is what makes a script sound like a computer.
- Emotion tags still start the line (`[excited]`, `[funny]`, ...).

## Before and after

> **Before:** It is the year twenty thirty-one. Gates to monster dungeons have opened in every
> city on Earth. Hunters go in, and they come out rich.
>
> **After:** So it's twenty thirty-one, and there are monster Gates in every city on Earth.
> Hunters walk in, fight whatever's inside, and walk out rich. Well... the ones who walk out.

> **Before:** This is Arjun. He is eighteen years old, and his hunter rank is F, the lowest rank
> there is. Business is slow.
>
> **After:** And this is Arjun. Eighteen, rank F, which is the lowest rank they've got, so
> instead of fighting monsters, he sells them tea. Business? Not great.

> **Before:** Hello friends, I'm Manoj. In this video, I'll tell you how one pig started the Pig
> War, what happened next, and who won in the end. Every word is true, and the sources are in the
> description.
>
> **After:** Hello friends, I'm Manoj. Grab your tea, because this one has a farmer, a very hungry
> pig, and America and the British Empire getting ready for war. Over one pig. And yes, it really
> happened.

## Shorts that travel: what a fast-growing channel does (studied 21 Nov 2026)

Stories Unarchived (@storiesunarchived, Indian mythology and temples, English, started 22 Oct 2026) reached 1.74
million views and 3,330 subscribers with 11 Shorts in its first month. We read all 11 scripts. What its winners do,
and its losers don't:

| Their video | Views | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Is This the World's First Shiva Temple? | 1,360,091 | a real place + a mystery question |
| This Temple Tells the Time of Year With Sunlight | 153,354 | a real place + a surprising fact |
| Was Krishna Right to Break the Rules of War? | 123,813 | a debate: three cases, then the question |
| 8 Shiva Temples. One Straight Line? | 119,366 | a claim the viewer checks ("Open Google Maps") |
| The Lakshmana Rekha Isn't in Valmiki's Ramayana | 13,877 | a myth-buster |
| 5 Deadliest Astras | 11,190 | a countdown |
| Jatayu vs Ravana / Ravana's Worst Mistake / The Bow No King Could Lift | 2,647 to 8,733 | a famous story retold |

1. **Length: 100 to 185 words, 40 to 63 seconds.** Nothing longer.
2. **Line 1 is the whole promise, with a concrete detail:** a number, a place, a name, and something that goes
   against what people believe. "A 700-year-old temple in India can reveal the time of year using nothing but
   sunlight." "Even Krishna's own brother called it a foul." "The famous Lakshmana Rekha isn't actually in Valmiki's
   Ramayana." "What if I told you there's a Shiva idol carved from a single emerald? And you can only see it once a
   year." Or an instruction the viewer can follow: "Open Google Maps. Find Kedarnath Temple."
3. **Real and checkable beats retold.** A real place, a real text, a real number the viewer can look up, or a
   question with two sides. A famous story told again ("Rama lifted the bow... CRACK!") gets a hundred times fewer
   views: everyone already knows the ending.
4. **Short sentences and fragments.** "No line drawn in the dirt. No magical boundary." "Below the waist."
5. **One turn in the middle:** "But here's what makes this truly fascinating", "And here's the surprising part".
6. **Honest words for what is not proven:** "Some believe", "According to legend", "According to local tradition".
   (Our rule anyway: a theory is called a theory.)
7. **The last line is an open question with two answers**, so people comment: "Could this really be the first
   Shiva temple in the world? Or is it a legend that has survived for thousands of years?" "So was it dharma? Or
   cheating?" "Think another astra deserves No. 1? Comment below." Their four biggest end with a question; their
   retellings end with a moral or "Jai Shri Ram", and get the fewest comments.
8. **No greeting, no channel name, no "subscribe"** inside the script.
9. **The title is the question; line 1 is the claim.** Title "Is This the World's First Shiva Temple?", line 1 "What
   if I told you there's a Shiva idol carved from a single emerald?".
10. **Tags are the place and the thing people search:** "uthirakosamangai temple", "emerald nataraja", "first shiva
    temple".

## Office humour that lands: what Tamil office videos do (studied 22 Nov 2026)

We read the scripts of 17 popular Tamil office videos from the last year: sketches, stand-up, narrated stories and micro-dramas. We also took their public numbers through the YouTube API (videos/office/audience.py). Likes per 1,000 views shows how much people enjoyed a video. Comments per 1,000 views shows how much it made them reply. Comment text could not be read: that needs a YouTube API key, which we don't have.

| Kind | Example (views) | Likes / 1k | Comments / 1k |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sketch built on one slang word | Mallesh Kannan, "நீயும் ஒரு சொம்புடா" (4.9 M) | 54.9 | 0.33 |
| Sketch with Chennai slang and a rhyme | Mallesh Kannan, "IT Pullingo" (1.8 M) | 62.6 | 0.22 |
| Literal-answer wordplay | "Office time … twist comedy" (1.8 M) | 43.3 | 0.15 |
| Repeated line, then a flip | Roll the Script, "Editor Pressure 2" (1.9 M) | 35.8 | 0.09 |
| Narrated story with clever, real know-how | Parithi, "தீர்வு" (0.8 M) | 38.1 | 0.47 |
| Story where viewers sort themselves | APPLEBOX, "நீங்க புத்திசாலியா?" (0.5 M) | 27.9 | 0.45 |
| Title asks about the viewer's office | Tech SuperStar, "உங்க Office'ல என்ன Game…" (2.3 M) | 59.2 | 2.80 |
| Undercover-owner micro-drama | "Office-ல அவனை அசிங்கப்படுத்தினாங்க… OWNER" (0.5 M) | 8.5 | 0.02 |
| "Fired, then promoted" story | Nenjil Unarvu, "Boss ஏன் அவரை…" (0.4 M) | 18.4 | 0.13 |

What the liked ones do with words:

1. **One local word or office phrase is the engine.** "சொம்பு" (sucking up) comes back more than ten times and ends as a new name: "இனிமேல் என் பேரு பாலா இல்ல, சொம்பு பாலா". The hearer waits for it each time.
2. **A list that escalates to the absurd.** The list of who must suck up to whom goes rich man, god, politician, husband, influencer, then countries. Three to six items, with the last one the most absurd.
3. **Taking an everyday question literally**, the classic Tamil comedy pair. "எப்படி போகுது?" gets "லிட்டருக்கு 20 கொடுக்குது", and "வீட்ல எப்படி?" gets "ஒரு ரூம், ஒரு ஹால், ஒரு பாத்ரூம்". A pun lands in the same way: "கால்நடையா போறீங்களே" means both "on foot" and "cattle". The question comes back three times and the asker gets angrier each time.
4. **The same line in a rhythm, then a flip.** The manager asks "முடிச்சியா? பண்ணியா? செஞ்சியா?" while the editor answers "அப்பதான … சொன்னீங்க" four times. Then the flip: "நான் முதல்ல எந்த வேலையும் செய்யல".
5. **Callbacks and revenge.** A small detail planted early pays off at the end: the manager's height, 5.5, returns as "அரசம்பளமா? உங்க ஹைட் பை…".
6. **A fake-sad flip.** A long sad speech about the family suddenly turns honest and selfish: "எனக்காக அடிச்சேன்டா".
7. **Real prices and brands.** Meesho, Ajio and Zomato; an Anirudh song; ₹2 lakh against ₹20,000; 840 students; 10 pm.
8. **The lesson as a quotable two-half line or a rhyme, not a lecture.** "நான் பேசல சார், மெயில் பேசுது." "தப்பு பண்றது ஒரு விஷயம். மத்தவங்கள மிதிக்கிறது வேற விஷயம்." A gana rhyme: "அங்க பாரு காரு. காருக்குள்ள யாரு?" Turned back on the viewer: "கண்ணாடிய பாரு".
9. **Stories that teach something real** get liked more than plain twists. A tea seller frees a stuck iron box with hot water, the way stuck tumblers are freed at a tea stall, and it ends with an அறநெறிச்சாரம் verse: 38 likes per 1,000. The undercover-owner and framed-guard twists get millions of views but 8 to 26 likes per 1,000. People watch those to the end but don't love them.
10. **Make it easy to comment.** The most-commented video asks about the viewer's own office in its title. Another story asks viewers to place themselves among three kinds of people. A one-word reply works too ("கமெண்ட்ல சூப்பர்னு கமெண்ட் பண்ணுங்க").

What does not work: a scattered set of unrelated jokes, a lesson that starts "பாடம் இதுதான்", a hello in the first second, or a story everyone can guess.

## The story loop: stakes, one question, a fair twist, the next loop (27 Nov 2026)

The owner asked to try this ("can we try like this"), from a creator's "Story Loop" for Shorts. It is old story craft
with a new name, and it fits our Shorts and long videos alike:

1. **Stakes in the first 3 seconds.** A person to root for, what they can lose, and a clock. It has to matter to
   our viewer, an office worker in Chennai or Bengaluru, not a millionaire. "Ravi has 3 days till appraisals, and his
   wedding is in March" beats "Let's talk about appraisals".
2. **One big question by second 5.** One, not three: "So who gets the bigger hike?" The viewer stays for the
   answer.
3. **A fair head fake.** Set up the answer they expect (A), with real clues, then turn to B. That turn is the
   twist. The rule that keeps trust: in history the twist must be TRUE, never a fake shock. In an office story it
   must be FAIR, so that the clues were there when you watch again.
4. **The re-hook.** Close the loop and open the next one at once. In a long video that is rule 9 (a turn every 30
   to 40 seconds). In a Short the last line opens the next loop: tomorrow's story, the next part, or a two-sided
   question for the comments. **Never promise a part 2 we are not making.**

Our limits:
- the loop serves the story, never the other way round;
- no invented stakes in true history;
- no "comment X to get Y" bait;
- **Kiddo Corner gets none of the re-hook pressure:** a story for children ends warmly, with at most one gentle
  question.

Example, the first try (an Office Tea Talks Short on point p010, written 27 Nov):
videos/office/story_loop_example.md.

## Check before voicing

- Contractions everywhere they would be said.
- No three same-shape short sentences in a row.
- One reaction or opinion a minute, in Manoj's voice.
- No line that only describes the picture.
- Nothing copied from an earlier video except the greeting.
