## Who it is for

Tamil families, and English-speaking Hindus abroad and their children, who want the gods' stories told simply
and beautifully: to watch in the morning or evening, to show their children, to learn the story behind a temple
or a festival.

## How it sounds and looks (different from Tea Break)

- **Respectful and warm, never jokey.** No punchlines about the gods, no office jokes, no stings like a rimshot.
  Wonder, warmth and a little humour from the story itself (a child's question, a devotee's simple love) are fine.
- **A storyteller, not a lecturer:** "Long ago, on Mount Kailash..." Short, clear sentences. Manoj's AI voice,
  calm.
- **Sources are named.** Each story says where it comes from ("This story is told in the Thiruvilaiyadal
  Puranam", "as the people of Palani tell it"). These are scripture and temple stories, told as stories, never
  as dated history. Where versions differ, say so simply ("In another telling...").
- **Pictures:** rich traditional devotional art (warm temple colours, gold, soft light), the gods drawn in their
  traditional forms, attributes and colours (Murugan with his vel and peacock, Ganesha with his broken tusk and
  mouse...). Never comic, never a god in a silly pose, no violence shown (a battle is shown before or after).
- **Music:** original, calm Indian instruments (veena, flute, mridangam, temple bells, nadaswaram feel). Never film
  songs or someone else's devotional recordings (copyright).
- **End:** a gentle line for the comments ("Which story of Murugan did your grandmother tell you?").
- YouTube labels: "altered or synthetic content" ticked (AI voice and pictures); not "made for kids" (family
  audience).
