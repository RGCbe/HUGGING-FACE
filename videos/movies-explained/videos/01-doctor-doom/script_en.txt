[curious] You think Thanos was dangerous? Doctor Doom never needed six stones. In the 2015 comics, he took the whole multiverse and became its god. But how?
[mysterious] His name is Victor von Doom. Stan Lee and Jack Kirby created him in 1962, in Fantastic Four number five. And here's the thing: most villains have one trick. Doom has three.
[excited] One. He's a genius and a sorcerer at the same time. Doom is a master of science and of magic. He builds machines to match anything Reed Richards can make, and he learned sorcery in the mountains of Tibet. Iron Man has a suit. Doctor Strange has spells. Doom has both.
[serious] Two. He's a king. Doom rules Latveria, a small country in Europe, and as its head of state he has diplomatic immunity. Think about that. The heroes can't just arrest him. He can do terrible things abroad and fly home to his castle.
[mysterious] And it all started with a grudge. At university, an experiment of his blew up. Doom blamed his rival, Reed Richards, hid his scarred face behind an iron mask, and never forgave him. How bad was the scar? Depends who's telling it. Jack Kirby drew just a tiny scar. Later writers made it far worse.
[shocked] Three. He has actually won. In the 2015 comic Secret Wars, Doom took what was left of a broken multiverse, rebuilt it, and ruled it as its god. It took Reed Richards to finally bring him down.
[excited] Now, on December the eighteenth, Avengers: Doomsday brings him to the big screen. The Avengers, the Fantastic Four and the X-Men, all facing one man. And the man under the mask? Robert Downey Junior. The same actor who played Iron Man.
[warm] So is Doom just a villain, or a king who thinks he's the only one smart enough to save the world? Tell me in the comments.
