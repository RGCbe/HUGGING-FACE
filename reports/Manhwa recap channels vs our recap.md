# Fix the voice's delivery, not its depth

The top English manhwa recap channels all sound alike. **One calm, deep, measured male narrator** tells the story in the third person, in every video, often for two to four hours. Our recap of *The F-Rank Tea Seller* misses that sound. The reason is not that the voice is too high. The reason is that **the voice does not move**. Our clone copies an 11.6-second bank announcement. On one test line its pitch moves only about **3 semitones**, while a designed storyteller voice moved about **14** on the same line. It talks at **194 words a minute** while sounding, it never pauses before a punchline, and its emotion tags do nothing, because no emotion samples exist. Making the owner's voice deeper put the pitch in the right place (101-113 Hz), but it stayed flat and fast. So the real fix is a new reference recorded as a story told to a friend, six emotion samples, and pauses written into the script. The scripts need the same care: the new SCRIPT_STYLE.md bans the template lines, picture-describing lines and choppy rhythm that every one of our scripts has, yet the weekly writer is still told to copy the old Pig War example. Three plumbing problems come first, because they can stop or hide everything else: the YouTube key may expire today, **no video has a custom thumbnail** because the channel is not phone-verified, and analytics are switched off, so nobody can yet test the owner's belief that the voice is what keeps viewers. The order is: plumbing this week, voice and script before the recap goes public on 6 November, then pictures, sound and packaging, and the bigger format choices later.

## Leaders lock one calm, deep voice; ours copies a flat announcement

### Top channels use one narrator and never name the tool

Top recap channels do not say who or what is speaking. In 262 recap videos from 2025-2026, **no big channel credited a voice tool**. The only ElevenLabs mention was in a tutorial ([own API sample](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/search/list), [Albert AI tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQaMZDUHBBI)). Across 8,313 descriptions from 38 channels, only about 0.1% mention a voice tool or AI ([own analysis of the YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/list)). The best clue to the winning sound comes from copying. On Fish Audio, a community clone called "FRESH MANHWA" is described as a "calm and professional male voice with a deep, resonant tone", "clear and measured, with a touch of mystery". **More than 3,400 creators have used it** ([Fish Audio](https://fish.audio/m/7714042439c24509ba4ef61c53671799/)). Manhwa Fresh is the leading long-form manhwa channel: 645K subscribers, a median of 398,469 views per video in 2025-26, about 2.3 videos a week, median length 234 minutes ([Manhwa Fresh](https://www.youtube.com/@manhwa_fresh)). Two limits apply. The Fish Audio description comes from search snippets (the page returned 404), and no source says whether Manhwa Fresh's narrator is a human or an AI.

Vendor guides push a different sound. FluxNote's 2026 guide wants "an enthusiastic, fast-paced narration voice" at 1.1x speed, "like a fan talking to a fan" ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/manhwa-webtoon-recap-channel)). The most-watched channel is described as calm instead. On one point everyone agrees: **one voice for the whole channel**. Recapo warns that "voice drift" between episodes makes "the channel feel less like a channel", and advises locking the model and version and keeping a pronunciation list ([Recapo](https://recapo.ai/blog/ai-narration-for-movie-recaps/)).

The owner's second reference video is a different format. Akatsuki's RomCom Mangas is a **manga dub**, not a recap. Lab316 Inc. localises Japanese manga videos with "dubbing by local voice actors" ([Lab316](https://lab316.me/)). The English channel has 95.8K subscribers and 839 videos ([Akatsuki EN](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCuHA9fYQn63yCN-UZ0Q2Ybg)), and the reference video runs 21 minutes ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZ8X1glKoMM)). Dubs run 13-31 minutes, speak in the first person, and give each character an actor. Recaps run for hours with one third-person narrator ([Kanon's RomCom Mangas](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCG0bIjs1DXYKvaCO4t6irAQ), [Manhwa Fresh reference video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OSXs52aICI)). What the two formats share is a **performed voice**: range, timing and character colour. One engineer puts the trap plainly: "A custom voice only solves timbre, not performance." The usual AI failures are every sentence with the same pitch shape, one energy level, and pauses of equal length. They are fine for 30 seconds and tiring after a few minutes ([dev.to](https://dev.to/julianbrown/why-ai-voices-sound-incredible-for-30-seconds-and-unbearable-after-three-minutes-1mfi)). A Japanese production guide says narration largely decides a manga video's quality and that viewers may leave partway when it is poor ([imitsu](https://imitsu.jp/matome/movie/howtomake-cartoonvideos)). Viewers also punish flat AI voices: Amazon pulled AI anime dubs after ridicule ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/amazon-removes-ai-generated-anime-070348147.html)), and one dub channel promised "NO MORE AI VOICES" ([Anime Therapist](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCGaZzFFejptMje4j93BaUvg)).

### Our clone moves 3 semitones on a line; a storyteller moves 14

Two terms first. **Hertz (Hz)** says how high a voice is. **Semitones** say how much it moves: one semitone is one step on a piano, and twelve steps make an octave. A voice that stays inside three steps sounds flat. The voice tests made in this session used the same opening line for every voice.

| Voice sample (same opening line) | Median pitch | Pitch range (10th-90th percentile) | Movement in semitones | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Our current clone of Manoj | 127 Hz | 119-140 Hz | 2.8 | 151 wpm |
| Manoj "deep" (reference pitched down) | 113 Hz | 103-132 Hz | 4.3 | 183-194 wpm before slowing |
| Manoj "deep", 1.7B model | 109 Hz | 101-122 Hz | 3.3 | 183-194 wpm before slowing |
| Manoj "deeper" | 101 Hz | 94-116 Hz | 3.6 | 183-194 wpm before slowing |
| B. Designed deep storyteller (Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign) | 94 Hz | 80-179 Hz | **13.9** | 137 wpm |
| C. Designed hype narrator | 232 Hz | 172-288 Hz | 8.9 | 138 wpm |
| D. Dub scene, four designed character voices | n/a | 85-372 Hz across all four | 25.6 | n/a |

Source: voice tests in this session (autocorrelation pitch tracker and Whisper). The semitone column is my calculation, 12 × log2(high ÷ low).

The full recap tells the same story. Across its 29 lines, the per-line median pitch stays between **123.5 and 141.1 Hz**. The voice talks at 150 words a minute overall, but at **194 words a minute while it is actually sounding**. The gaps sit almost only between lines (0.5 s each). Inside lines there are only 43 pauses of 0.12 s or more, with a median of 0.28 s. There is **no pause at all before "cardamom", "Oh no", "They run" or "to go?"**, the punchlines. **All 7 question lines end flat or falling**, so they do not sound like questions. The emotion tags change nothing: "excited", "shocked" and "funny" lines measure the same as untagged lines (pitch p=0.51, loudness p=0.92), and the "excited" lines have the lowest pitch of all ([timing.json](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/ep01/recap/timing.json), [line audio](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/ep01/recap/tts/)). Two spots may be glitches and need a listen: a squeak on "it?" in "Did he just one-shot it?" (about 358 Hz for 0.4 s) and a sound after "rich." in line 3 ([our recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md)).

The cause is the reference recording. The clone learns from one file, ref_cut.wav: **11.61 seconds, 33 words of a bank announcement**. That file has a median pitch of 128.6 Hz, a narrow range (4.6 semitones from the 5th to the 95th percentile), a pace of 170.5 words a minute, and **one pause of 0.18 s in the whole clip**. The clone copies it almost exactly: 127.9 Hz and 4.5 semitones in the Pig War ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)). The emotion samples that should vary the delivery do not exist. The recap's `refs.json` is empty and its log says "emotion samples: none yet (every line in the usual voice)" ([refs.json](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/ep01/recap/tts/refs.json), [voice.log](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/ep01/recap/voice.log)). Yet the weekly skill tells the writer that "The voice is made from the owner's own sample of that emotion" ([SKILL.md](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/.claude/skills/tea-break-weekly/SKILL.md)). Today that sentence is not true.

### Going deeper fixed the pitch, not the melody

Pitch was a real gap, and the owner's choice fixes it. Adult male speech sits at about **85-155 Hz** ([Wikipedia: voice frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_frequency)), and a "deep" narrator sits in the lower half. In a 2012 lab study with made-up election candidates, listeners chose the lower-pitched version of the same voice about 60% of the time and rated it more competent and trustworthy ([LiveScience](https://www.livescience.com/19028-voice-pitch-presidential-elections.html)). At 101-113 Hz, all three deep versions land in the target band.

But the movement did not grow. The deep versions move only **3.3-4.3 semitones**, and they talk at 183-194 words a minute before slowing. The clone copies *how* the reference speaks, not only how high. A flat recording pitched down becomes a deep flat voice. Slowing it evenly makes it slower, but the pauses still fall in the same wrong places. The designed storyteller (B) proves that the same engine can move about 14 semitones when the delivery asks for it. So the bottleneck is the delivery in the reference recording, not the engine and not the pitch.

Which deep version should we keep? "Deeper" is about 4.2 semitones below the reference, "deep" about 2.2, and the 1.7B version about 2.9 (my calculation from the medians). The research notes give a working rule, built from vendor limits rather than measured: **do not pitch a voice down by more than about 2-3 semitones**, or artefacts creep in ([narration notes](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/narration_and_audio.md); Azure keeps pitch changes "within 0.5 to 1.5 times the original", [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-synthesis-markup-voice)). So "deep", ideally with the 1.7B model if it sounds clean, is safer than "deeper". Pitching down also darkens a tone that is already dark: the clone's 2-5 kHz "presence" band sits about 29 dB below its low band. The audit called this darker than usual but asked for a check against a good human recording before acting ([our recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md)). One more thing: the pipeline still points `VOICE_REF` at the old ref_cut.wav ([env.sh](file:///root/.cache/tea-break/env.sh), checked 30 Oct), so the deep choice is not wired in yet.

Is the voice really what makes viewers stay? The evidence leans the owner's way for **staying after the click**: the Japanese guide, the AI-voice backlash, and 3,400 creators copying one narrator's voice all point there. But nothing on our channel can test it. The Analytics API is off, so there are no retention curves. And the two Emu War Shorts used the same voice, style and story, yet got **179 and 19 views** ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)). Topic, hook and packaging decide the click. My position: the voice decides whether people stay, packaging decides whether they come, and right now the packaging gives the voice no chance.

### How to rebuild the voice, step by step

The spec below compares a target for our narrator with what we measured. The targets are starting points built from long-form voiceover data, audiobook craft and TTS documentation, not measurements of top channels, which nobody has published.

| What | Target for our narrator | Ours today | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | One narrator, Manoj, locked across every video | Same clone in history and recap | Keep. Lock the model, version, reference and settings. Re-voice one fixed test line each run and compare it. |
| Pitch | About 90-120 Hz | 127-129 Hz | "Deep" (109-113 Hz); shift no more than about 3 semitones |
| Movement on a line | Wide; the designed storyteller reached 13.9 semitones | 2.8 semitones | New storyteller reference. A first gate of 6+ semitones (my starting number, to tune by ear) |
| Speed | 155-170 wpm overall; 130-150 for tension and sad beats; 175-190 for fights | 150 overall, but 194 while sounding; the closing ask at 198 | Speak slower in the reference itself; avoid even time-stretching |
| Pauses | Sentence 0.4-0.6 s; story beat 0.75-1 s; before a reveal 1-1.5 s; after a punchline 0.5-1 s; scene change 1.5-2 s | 0.5 s after every line; 0.28 s median inside lines; none before punchlines | Write pause marks into the script; the pipeline inserts exact silences |
| Questions | Rise at the end | All 7 fall or stay flat | Put a real rising question into the new recordings |
| Emotion | Calm base; quieter and closer for tension; lighter for comedy | Tags have no effect | Record the six emotion samples |
| Noise | Noise floor below -60 dB | Reference -43.8 dBFS; Manoj's own "my take" clip -37.2 dBFS | Quiet room; clean the my-take clips before mixing |

Sources: long-form median of 166.5 wpm ([OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-script-length-word-count)); faster for fights, slower for sad or tense scenes ([audiobook tips](https://cchogan.dancingbear.co.uk/audiobook-tips-a-short-guide/audiobook-tips-reading/)); standard pause lengths of 500, 750, 1,000 and 1,250 ms ([Microsoft SSML](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-synthesis-markup-structure)); noise floor and consistency rules ([ACX](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-audio-submission-requirements)); our measurements from the [recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md) and the [whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md).

The recording brief is simple. Manoj records a new main reference of about the same length as now, in a quiet room with no fan or traffic. He does not read a statement. He **tells a bit of a story to a friend**: low and relaxed, slower before the reveal, a full one-second pause, a question that really goes up, a small smile on the joke. His casual my-take clip moved only about 5 semitones, so normal talk is not enough: the storytelling must be a little bigger than feels natural. Then he records six short clips, one per emotion tag the pipeline already knows: excited, mysterious, funny, shocked, serious and warm. build.py already looks for them as `teabreak/emotion/<tag>.opus` plus a `.txt` transcript in the private voice dataset ([build.py](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/teabreak/build.py), [config.json](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/teabreak/config.json)). All seven files get the same pitch shift. Then the pipeline gets a **voice check**: it measures pitch, movement, speed while sounding and the pause before each punch word, and stops the run if a line falls outside the targets. This is how the "voice drift" problem gets caught before upload.

Character voices come later. Test D shows the engine can make four distinct voices (85-372 Hz), and a hybrid "Manhwa Recap Dub" format exists: Losweko's ten-minute one has 650K views ([Losweko](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Og8kH4_NFIk)). Recap tools already split "different voices for narration vs. dialogue" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/aashish124/how-i-built-an-ai-studio-that-turns-manhwa-chapters-into-narrated-recap-videos-5enl)). For us, that means Manoj narrates and Viktor, Meera and Arjun each say one or two key lines per scene in their own voice. Do this only after the narrator is fixed. Flat AI character voices are exactly what viewers mocked in Amazon's dubs. The hype narrator (C, 232 Hz) is above the normal male speaking range and does not match the calm sound the leader is known for, so I would not use it for narration.

## Recap scripts are written for the ear; ours describe the picture

### What top recap scripts do

Recap scripts are built to be heard. The hook comes in the first 3-15 seconds and rests on a contradiction or a reversal. Recapo contrasts "A man who was never late in his life just missed the last train on purpose" with "Today I'm going to tell you about a thriller". It lists "ending-first" as one of five hook types, asks for a small "jolt every 30–40 seconds (a new question, a reversal, or a one-line take)", and names "But then, he discovers a shocking secret" as a sign of generic AI writing. It says to end with "a stance, not a summary" ([Recapo script guide](https://recapo.ai/blog/how-to-write-a-movie-recap-script/)). A 2026 manhwa guide calls "Today, I will explain this manhwa" a weak opener ([Techgili](https://techgili.com/how-to-build-a-manhwa-recap-channel-that-can-earn-10-per-1000-views/)). RecapManga says to lead with reaction rather than a panel-by-panel reading, and to attribute quoted lines so the narration is not "a wall of unattributed quotes" ([RecapManga](https://recapmanga.com/blog/manhwa-recap-scripts-ai-guide)). English recaps use the third person ("He…"); first person belongs to Hindi recaps and dubs ([Manhwa Fresh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gecmpeZXDos), [yuva explainop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MbGvwZqwM14)). Written hooks show the reversal style: "But our MC is a blank page. That is until…" ([Akiel Runes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAPhcHn0rDM)). ElevenLabs adds a voice-specific point: explicit tags like "she whispered" make TTS emotion more predictable ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices)).

### Where our recap script falls short

Some parts are right. The recap is in the third person and present tense. It opens on a real contradiction ("This boy is the weakest hunter on Earth… But anyone who drinks his tea becomes unstoppable, for exactly one hour"), it has six joke lines, and it ends on a cliffhanger ("Something has noticed his tea."). The problems are in how the lines are built for the voice ([recap.json](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/ep01/recap/recap.json), [our recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md)).

The greeting takes 8 seconds (11.6-19.5 s), so **the story only starts at 0:20**. Lines like "It is the year twenty thirty-one" and "This is Arjun. He is eighteen…" are the exact "Before" examples that SCRIPT_STYLE.md rewrites, and the script has 7 uncontracted forms. **About 9 of 29 lines only describe the picture**, and for **11 of 20 speech bubbles the narrator says the same words** that are on screen. Dialogue is reported in the same voice after "says:", and three lines have no speaker. Worse, the punchlines are readable **1-4 seconds before they are spoken**, and "I'm undercharging" 8.4 seconds before. The closing ask squeezes 34 words into 10.3 seconds (198 wpm) and never points to another video. Our own hook scorer rates the hook 34 ("WEAK"). That score should not be chased, though: the scorer rewards "you", a date and "But how?", which is how the history videos ended up with one formula.

The comic also has a better opening that the recap skipped. Panel 1 shows Viktor glowing gold, asking "This power… where is it coming from?!", and panel 2 says "One hour earlier…" ([episode.json](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/ep01/episode.json)). That is an **ending-first hook, already drawn**. Here is an illustration of an opening written to SCRIPT_STYLE.md, with proposed pause marks in brackets:

> [mysterious] Viktor Hale is glowing gold, and he has no idea why. [pause 1.0] One hour ago, he was laughing at a tea boy.
> [funny] That tea boy is Arjun. Rank F, the weakest hunter on Earth, so he sells chai next to the Gate. Ten rupees a glass. [pause 0.6] And anyone who drinks it becomes unstoppable… [pause 1.0] for exactly one hour.
> [warm] Hello friends, I'm Manoj, and this is our own webtoon.

The greeting shrinks to about 3 seconds and comes after the hook, which is where the leaders' rule ("no channel intro before the hook") and our brand meet.

### The history scripts repeat one template, and the writer still copies it

Every history video opens with "Before you (start) laugh(ing), this is real history", then "In this video, I'll tell you how [X] started, what happened next, and who won in the end", and ends with the same comments line. Outside the greeting, the Zanzibar script has **0 contractions** and the Pig War **1**. The Emu War has a run of **9 short sentences in a row**. When the listening test failed a Zanzibar line, the fix was to chop it in two ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)). SCRIPT_STYLE.md now bans all of these: "Contractions, always", "Never three short sentences of the same shape in a row", "Tell the story, don't describe the picture", "Fresh words every video", and "Don't chop it into shorter sentences" ([SCRIPT_STYLE.md](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/SCRIPT_STYLE.md)).

The rules now apply to all scripts, but the weekly writer's source material still contradicts them. On 30 October, the example voiceover still contains "In this video, I'll tell you how one pig started the Pig War…" ([example/voiceover_en.txt](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/teabreak/example/voiceover_en.txt)). The skill still says "The finished example to copy is The Pig War" and still offers "Before you laugh, this is real history" as its model hook ([SKILL.md](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/.claude/skills/tea-break-weekly/SKILL.md)). The scheduled task's prompt, per the audit, says "Copy the files in videos/teabreak/example/ (The Pig War) for the shape of every file you write". Unless the example is rewritten, **the 4 November run will probably repeat the template**.

### Writing the voice's directions into the script

The script is the only place where we can direct a TTS voice, so the directions must live in the text. SCRIPT_STYLE.md already says that punctuation is direction and that the stressed word goes last. The missing piece is **timed pauses**. The research advice is to generate sentence by sentence and insert exact silences, rather than trusting the model's own pauses ([narration notes](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/narration_and_audio.md)). Our pipeline already cuts lines into pieces and joins them with fixed 0.2 s and 0.5 s silences ([build.py](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/teabreak/build.py)). A pause mark such as `[pause 1.0]` could set that gap instead. The mark must be removed before the text reaches the voice, because the skill warns that sound tags get read out. With pause marks, a real rising question, one quoted line per scene "with attitude and who said it", and one honest reaction from Manoj about once a minute, the script gives the new voice something to perform.

## Sound, pictures and packaging: right levels, wrong details

### The mix is on target, but the music never rests

Leaders keep music low and simple. Few credit their music; those that do use free lo-fi, chill hip-hop or light pop beds ([Gido Cuni Mahwa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JMw7NsRdW3E), [Schikan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b4KL405WjQE)). Guides put music about 18-24 dB under the voice, and W3C sets at least 20 dB as a safe floor ([Umbrella Creators](https://umbrellacreators.com/blog/background-music-volume-under-voiceover), [W3C G56](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG20/Techniques/general/G56)). YouTube turns loud uploads down toward about -14 LUFS ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_normalization)). Sound effects are expected but sparse; one creator promised that "the next recap will have sound effects as well!" ([KenRecaps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZJ1ub_26s8A)).

Our levels pass. The recap is mastered to **-14.3 LUFS**, and during speech the voice leads the music by a median of 30 dB. The music is original and generated note by note, so nothing can match it in a copyright claim ([score.py](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/doodle/score.py)). The weak points are elsewhere. **The music never stops**, except for a 1.4-second drop under "cardamom", and no stretch of the recap ever falls below -40 dBFS. The music changes mood **5 times a minute** (17 segments in 3:10), and the "sad" cue was dropped because it was shorter than 3 seconds. The Pig War has 15 mood segments, against the skill's own "5 to 8 moods". The comic's drawn sound words (KRASH, SLASH, GRRAAAH) make no sound ([our recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md), [whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)). Change music at story acts, not scenes. Allow a real second of silence before the biggest reveal. Give each drawn sound word and the "[ HIDDEN SKILL ]" window one quiet effect. Denoise Manoj's real my-take clip, which is about 7 dB noisier than the reference and is spliced in raw.

### The pictures stand still 72% of the time and give punchlines away

Leaders mix two layouts: a tall panel centred over a blurred copy of itself (Mamoru, Tobs), and wide panels cropped to fill the frame (Manhwa Fresh), often in the same video ([Mamoru still](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/YGLNNUaMju0/hq1.jpg), [Manhwa Fresh still](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/pjOoN03xa0k/hq1.jpg), [ManhwaDealer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/U-k_Mr8q1sk/hq1.jpg)). Editors describe the toolkit as "dynamic cuts, subtle zooms and pans, dramatic sound design, clean subtitles" ([Freelancer brief](https://www.freelancer.com/projects/video-post-editing/manhwa-recap-video-editing)). Burned-in narration subtitles are **rare in long-form**: in about 48 stills from 16 long videos, only one channel showed them. They are a Shorts habit ([visuals notes](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/visuals_structure_policy.md), [Evergarden](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/obaZWVPr2Zc/hq1.jpg)).

Our recap uses the centred-strip layout (the comic fills 52% of the frame), which is normal. But **the picture is completely still in about 72% of frames**, with no zooms, and the longest still stretch is 17.5 seconds. Burned-in captions cover lettering in 7 of 36 sampled frames. Nothing on screen names the series or the channel. The last 13.3 seconds are one frozen "TO BE CONTINUED" frame that cannot even be centred ([our recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md), [recap.py](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/recap.py)). The fixes follow from the measurements. Add a slow zoom to every held panel. Show the series logo in the first seconds. For the 16:9 video, upload the captions as a YouTube caption track instead of burning them over the art (the channel uploads none today). Most of all, stop the bubbles from spoiling jokes. Because our own pipeline letters the strip from episode.json, it should be able to render each panel clean and pop each bubble in when the voice reaches it. The history videos have a milder version of the same issue: a board every 10 seconds, and boards that write out the template outline ("1. How it started 2. What happened next 3. Who won?").

### The Short and TikTok parts lose the story at the edges

Recap Shorts are mostly 30-60 seconds of full-screen close-ups with one- to three-word bold captions, a watermark, and a title reveal at the end ([Manhwa Tease](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/ksO7HaHbhJk/oar2.jpg)). Our 44-second Short fits the length. But it **never says "one hour"**, so its payoff ("the clock reaches sixty minutes") has no set-up, and it names Viktor and Arjun without introducing them. Its last move is a 0.8-second blur back through 32,700 pixels of strip, and it has no on-screen title or end card. The three TikTok parts are better packaged ("PART n/3" label and a cover end card), but parts 2 and 3 start mid-story with no "previously" line, and part 3 still says "The link is in the description", which is YouTube wording ([our recap audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/our_recap_audit.md)). A 2026 series guide advises opening each part with a short recap for people who missed the last one ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/agentworkshop/how-to-plan-the-next-part-of-a-tiktok-series-so-part-2-viewers-arent-lost-2026-recap-callback-e1a)). The history TikTok video also puts its captions at about y=1550 of 1920, under TikTok's own bottom overlay. The recap's overlay fix was never copied to it ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)).

### No video has a custom thumbnail yet

Leaders' titles are long premise sentences: a median of 87 characters, often ending " - Manhwa Recap" (51%), with ALL-CAPS words (43%) and numbers (43%), and almost never emojis. Single title features move views by only about 10-20%; **the story premise matters more** ([own analysis of 3,520 titles](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/list)). Thumbnails are bright redrawn scenes with 2-5 characters, yellow ALL-CAPS labels with black outlines, arrows, rank labels like "F-RANK HUNTER", game-style pop-ups and before/after splits ([Frieren Manhwa](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/6nW6LZJjLZE/maxresdefault.jpg), [ManhwaDealer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/xfiuZGDZJoU/maxresdefault.jpg), [Lumos](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/zg6aDKUSvYE/maxresdefault.jpg)).

Our biggest packaging problem is not design. **None of our 13 videos shows a custom thumbnail.** YouTube only allows them on verified accounts ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431)), our channel is not verified, and every upload shows a frame of the video instead. For the recap, that frame is a dark wolf panel with the caption "Inside the dungeon," ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)). Our designed recap thumbnail is also weaker than the leaders' style: two panel crops and "HIS TEA = 1 HOUR OF POWER", with no tea glass, no monster, no rank label and no series name. A stronger version would show Arjun with an "F-RANK" label, a glowing glass of chai, and Viktor glowing gold with a game-style "BUFF: 1 HOUR" box, in bright colours. The title "His Tea Gives You 1 Hour of Power | The F-Rank Tea Seller, Episode 1" (68 characters) could follow the niche formula, for example "The WEAKEST Hunter Sells Tea, but One Glass Makes Anyone UNSTOPPABLE for 1 Hour - Webtoon Recap" (95 characters).

The description is where we already beat the niche. Leaders hide the series name ("Name in the pinned comment"), stuff tags with rival channel names, and credit nobody: only 3.6% of 8,313 descriptions credit an author or platform, and 0.5% link an official reading page ([own analysis](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/list), [Manhwa Fresh example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Io4fQN_qSM)). Ours names the series, links WEBTOON, credits Manoj and explains the AI use. Keep that, and add what is still missing. That means a series playlist (the recap was rightly pulled out of the true-history playlists and now sits in none), chapters (YouTube needs at least three timestamps from 00:00, each 10 seconds or longer, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)), and an end screen set in Studio. Other small fixes: the Zanzibar title lacks "Zanzibar", the Pig War and Zanzibar Shorts do not link the full video, the recap pair is set to `embeddable: false`, and two public playlists are empty ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)).

## A three-minute original fits an empty lane, if the rules are kept

### No English competitor posts recaps under five minutes

In 3,662 long-form uploads from 20 English recap channels in 2025-26, the median length is **162 minutes**, and **not one** is under 5 minutes. The 5-30 minute videos get 0.66 times their channel's usual views, while 10-hour-plus "complete" compilations get 1.63 times. That second number is an upper bound, because channels compile stories that are already popular. Shorts posted by long-form channels get only 0.14-0.44 times their usual views, while Shorts-only channels thrive with Shorts of about 57 seconds ([own analysis](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/list)). The only short formats that work at scale are close to ours. Dummyworld's Korean 3-4 minute summaries with original characters reach a median of 855,694 views, and its 13-14 minute compilations do 2.14 times its median ([Dummyworld](https://www.youtube.com/@dummyworld_kr1)). skipos's 4-5 minute English comic dubs, with credited voice actors, do 1.68 times its median ([skipos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6b2Z21TAWXg)).

A webtoon with one episode cannot be a two-hour recap, so the three-minute episode is the right unit for now. The plan should be weekly episode videos of 3-5 minutes, one 30-60 second hook Short per episode, and a longer "season so far" video every five episodes or so. That compilation should add new narration rather than re-upload the same cut again and again, because repeated cumulative re-uploads look like the "repetitive" content YouTube targets. One open question is the channel itself. Tea Break with Manoj promises funny *true* history in its name, description and keywords, while the recap is fiction ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)). The leaders are all single-subject channels. With 8 subscribers the cost of mixing is small today. I would decide after three or four episodes, using analytics, whether the series should move to a "Tea Break Comics" channel that matches the WEBTOON name. This is my judgement, not a finding.

### Leaders never miss a week, and our promise is already at risk

Leaders post on a fixed clock. Manhwa Fresh posts 2.3 long videos a week; Mamoru, Tobs, Frieren and Lumos had **no empty week** in the last 26; the 2026 newcomers that grew fastest post almost daily. English channels cluster in two slots, about 13:00-15:00 UTC and 21:00-01:00 UTC. The rhythm a solo creator can copy is Dummyworld's: one weekly video in a fixed slot ([own analysis](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/playlistItems/list), [Lumos](https://www.youtube.com/@lumosmanhwarecap), [Dummyworld](https://www.youtube.com/@dummyworld_kr1)). Our history slot (Saturday, Monday and Wednesday at 18:00 IST, which is 12:30 UTC) sits right next to the US-morning slot. The recap breaks it, with a Friday video and a Sunday Short that put five uploads into 6-11 November. The bigger risk is the promise. The recap, the Short, the WEBTOON page and the creator bio all say **"a new episode every Thursday"**, but no Episode 2 exists in the project ([f-rank folder](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/videos/manga/f-rank/), checked 30 Oct). If 5 November passes without one, the recap goes public a day later with a promise already broken ([WEBTOON series](https://www.webtoons.com/en/canvas/the-f-rank-tea-seller/list?title_no=1182633)).

### Owning the comic removes the copyright risk, not the template risk

Recapping someone else's manhwa is getting riskier. In July 2026 a US judge found that anime recaps which "describe and narrate" the works were not fair use, in KADOKAWA's subpoena case. That ruling is not a final decision and is under appeal ([TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/court-rejects-fair-use-claim-orders-youtube-to-unmask-anime-recap-channels/)). YouTube's reused-content rule bars "readings of other materials you did not originally create" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). Our webtoon is ours: WEBTOON CANVAS creators keep ownership ([CANVAS terms](https://www.webtoons.com/en/terms/canvasTermsOfUsePolicy)). YouTube also does not require AI disclosure for "cloning your own voice for voiceovers" or for fantastical animated scenes, and says that disclosing "alone won't limit a video's audience" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). We disclose more than we must, which is fine.

What remains is the **inauthentic-content rule**. Since July 2025 it bars "templated storylines" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). TechCrunch reported a July 2026 clarification aimed at template content that does not "really have a narrative arc", though YouTube's Help changelog does not list it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). An unattended weekly job that reuses the same opener, greeting, outline board and comments line is close to that wording. Manoj's own recorded takes are the strongest proof of human input, so every video should carry at least one, cleaned. One ad rule also matters for a monster comic: graphic violence "in the thumbnail or in the first seven seconds" loses ads, so the cold open and thumbnail should glow, not bleed ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278)).

Two pipeline risks sit underneath all of this. Google gives apps in "Testing" mode refresh tokens that expire after 7 days ([Google Identity](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)). Our key was connected on about 23 October at 10:45 UTC, so it may die **today**, 30 October. That would stop the 4 November weekly run at its second step and block every upload; videos already scheduled would still go public. The same key lacks the comments scope, and the YouTube Analytics API is switched off in the project. So the pipeline cannot see a single retention curve or comment ([whole-channel audit](file:///home/user/HUGGING-FACE/research_notes/Manhwa%20recap%20channels%20vs%20our%20recap/whole_channel_audit.md)).

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that our voice problem is one of **direction, not of the instrument**. The same engine produced a 14-semitone storyteller when asked to. Our clone is flat because we asked it to copy a bank announcement and gave it no other samples. Pitching that recording down made it deeper without making it alive. So the biggest voice fix costs a few minutes of recording and a few pause marks, not a new tool or a new voice. A second finding cuts the other way. The automation that lets one person ship a video every week is also the thing most likely to make every video sound the same. That sameness is what tires listeners, and it is what YouTube's inauthentic-content rule names. The weekly writer's example files therefore matter as much as the voice.

Our real edge over the leaders is ownership. They hide series names, credit nobody and now face court rulings, while we can name, credit, chapter and link openly in a lane no competitor uses. That edge only pays if people click, so a verified channel and real thumbnails come before any voice work goes public. Once analytics are on, the owner's belief about the voice stops being a belief. The first retention curve after 6 November will show whether viewers leave at the greeting, at the first flat stretch, or not at all.
