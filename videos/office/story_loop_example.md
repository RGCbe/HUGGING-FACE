# Story loop, first example: an Office Tea Talks Short (about 35 s)

Point: p010 in points.json ("Your hike depends a lot on how much money your project earns, not only on how hard you
work"). Invented characters; the description says it is a story. The numbers are story details, not statistics.

**Title:** He Worked Every Weekend. Who Got the Bigger Hike?

| Beat | Time | Line (English) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Stakes | 0-3 s | Three days to appraisals. Ravi worked forty-two weekends this year... and his wedding is in March. |
| 2. Big question | 3-6 s | Karthik, two desks away? Out at six, every single day. So... who gets the bigger hike? |
| Set-up for A (clues) | 6-15 s | Appraisal day. The manager smiles at Ravi. "Your effort this year? Amazing. Everyone saw it." Ravi texts his fiancée: "Book the hall." *(Clue, fair: Karthik's team "keeps getting new clients".)* |
| 3. Head fake | 15-21 s | Letters come out. Karthik: twelve percent. Ravi... three. |
| Why (the point) | 21-29 s | Karthik's project brought money in. Ravi's was a cost the company wanted to cut. On a cost project, overtime is invisible. |
| 4. Re-hook | 29-35 s | So Ravi asked his manager one question... and the answer changed his next year. Tomorrow. Which kind is YOUR project, earning or cost? |

Notes:
- The re-hook promises tomorrow's Short. Make it, with Ravi's question ("Which project here makes money?") and
  the manager's answer, or end only on the comment question instead.
- The comedy: the "Book the hall" text gives a beat of embarrassment, at Ravi's hope and never at his pain (the office humour rules).
- The Tamil version tells the same beats in spoken Tamil (never essay Tamil), written fresh, not translated line by line.
